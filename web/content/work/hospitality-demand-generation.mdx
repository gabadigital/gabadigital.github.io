## Context

A hospitality group with several locations across Lagos was posting whenever a manager had time, with no shared calendar and no consistent look across accounts. Ad spend went toward whichever post was live that week, with no link back to actual reservations.

## Problem

Each location's page looked different, campaigns were not planned around occupancy or events, and nobody could say which posts or ads were driving bookings versus just likes. Marketing spend and reservations lived in two different worlds.

## Approach

We set up a shared content calendar and a repeatable visual system so every location posted on-brand, on schedule, tied to real occasions: new menus, events, and slow nights that needed a push. Ad campaigns were rebuilt around a booking goal instead of reach, with budget shifted weekly toward whatever was converting.

## Result

Reservation bookings grew 37% while cost per booking dropped 26%, because spend followed what was actually converting instead of what looked good. Engagement rose 58% once every location shared one visual identity instead of five different ones.

> "For the first time, our marketing budget is tied to tables filled, not just likes."
